## Problem Statement
Small shops in Bhutan keep their books in paper ledgers or Excel. The original pitch was a full inventory and business management web app: products, stock, purchases, sales, expenses, customer credit, profit reports, and automatic 5% GST with receipts. The session narrowed that to one concrete pain: **the shop owner tracks customer credit (*baki*) in a paper ledger**. The ledger can't total balances, flag old debts, or remind customers to pay. A second possible pain, for GST-registered shops only, is issuing GST-correct invoices since GST began on 1 Jan 2026.

## Demand Evidence
None yet. The founder said: "I haven't personally spoken to a real shop owner in Bhutan or observed one struggling with inventory management. DrukStock is currently an idea." Every customer claim in this doc is a hypothesis until the interviews in The Assignment are done.

## Status Quo
The founder believes: "they use excel paper ledgers". What the workaround costs (time per day, Nu lost to forgotten credit, GST effort) is unknown and is the first thing the interviews must measure.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **Primary hypothesis:** "general grocery shop in Thimphu, owner tracks credit in ledger."
- **Competing hypothesis (from the second opinion):** a GST-registered Thimphu hardware or building-materials shop. These shops are likely above the Nu 5M turnover threshold, carry large contractor credit, and must issue GST invoices.
- **Which one is the first customer is OPEN.** The interviews decide.
- **Wedge for both:** credit tracking (who owes what, how long, a one-tap WhatsApp reminder). GST invoicing is the second module, gated on evidence.

## Constraints
- Users are non-technical and work at a busy counter, often one-handed on an Android phone.
- Connectivity at the counter can't be assumed, so recording an entry must work offline.
- It must beat a free paper ledger on speed: target under 5 seconds per credit entry.
- Bhutan GST: 5% flat since 1 Jan 2026. Registration is compulsory at turnover of Nu 5M or more and voluntary above Nu 2.5M. Staples such as rice, salt and cooking oil are exempt. (Sources: DRC FAQ, Business Bhutan. Whether the line is "at" or "above" Nu 5M must be confirmed against the Act.)
- Competitor: aBit POS (ABIT Pvt Ltd, iPad, launched May 2026) already ships Bhutan GST receipts with CID/TPN.

No user-facing screens are built until the Phase 0 decision rule says go. (Kept, with Approach B sequenced around it; see Recommended Approach.)
2. The first customer is a Thimphu shop owner who tracks credit on paper. **Revised:** grocery vs. GST-registered hardware is decided by the interviews.
3. The wedge is credit tracking plus reminders, not a full inventory/POS suite.
4. **Revised after the second opinion:** GST is not the wedge for small sub-Nu-5M grocers, but it may be the wedge for registered hardware/wholesale shops. Test, don't assume.
5. It runs on a phone the owner already has, with near-zero setup.

## Approaches Considered
- **A: Baki Book (minimal PWA, offline, no backend).** Recommended by the facilitator. Not chosen: the founder prefers to build the real foundation now.
- **C: Concierge (founder does the books for shops by hand via WhatsApp and a Google Sheet).** Not chosen: it doesn't test whether the app beats the ledger at the counter. Still usable as a one-shop GST test (see Open Questions).

### Approach B: DrukStock Core (CHOSEN)
A proper multi-tenant web app: phone-number login, a cloud database, sync across the owner's and staff's devices, and a module layout. Credit first, GST invoices second, inventory, sales and reports later. Effort L (human: ~3-4 months / CC: ~4-6 days plus testing; revised up for offline sync, roles and auth). Risk Med-High.

## Recommended Approach
**Approach B, chosen by the founder over the facilitator's recommendation of A.** Rationale: the founder intends to run this as a real company and wants a foundation that can hold accounts, staff and paid tiers.

To keep premise #1 honest, the build is **sequenced against evidence**:

**Phase 0 (week 1): interviews + foundation only.** 5 grocers and 5 GST-registered hardware shops (see The Assignment). Allowed build work this week: data model, tenant isolation, auth, sync layer. No screens.

**Phase 0 decision rule.** Two tests:
- **Grocer test:** ≥3 of 5 grocers show a credit ledger with debts older than 30 days.
- **Hardware test (the Phase 2 gate):** ≥3 of 5 registered hardware shops issue GST invoices by hand, in Excel or through an accountant, AND say they'd pay Nu 300-500/month for phone-based invoicing. Also record whether they carry customer credit.

| Result | First customer | What happens to the build |
|---|---|---|
| Both pass | Hardware (the one that pays); grocers as usage testers | Build Phase 1 screens, then Phase 2 right after |
| Grocer only | Grocers | Build Phase 1 screens; Phase 2 stays gated |
| Hardware only | Hardware | Build Phase 1 only if ≥3 of 5 hardware shops carry credit, otherwise go straight to Phase 2 on the foundation |
| Neither | None | Pause: no screens. Interview 10 more shops in a different segment (e.g. wholesalers, pharmacies) or revisit the idea before writing more code |

**Phase 1: Credit module (screens built only after the decision rule says go).**

*Data model:*
- Shop (name, TPN optional, mBoB/payment details, reminder message template, default country code +975) → users (owner, staff) → customers → ledger entries (credit / payment / reversal, amount in Nu, recorded_at, note, recorded_by, device_id).
- Customer phone numbers are stored in E.164 format (+975 plus 8 digits) so a reminder opens `wa.me/975XXXXXXXX`. Phone is optional; reminders are disabled for customers without one.
- Balances are derived from entries, never stored as a mutable total, so the books stay auditable.

*Sync model (offline-first):*
- Every record (entry, customer) gets a client-generated UUID. Uploads are idempotent upserts keyed on that UUID, so a retry never duplicates an entry.
- Balances are sums, so the order entries arrive in doesn't change the result. History shows the device's `recorded_at`; the server also stores `received_at`, which is used for ordering when device clocks disagree.
- The app requests persistent browser storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`). An "N entries not backed up" badge shows any unsynced entries, and the app warns when anything has been unsynced for more than 24 hours. A lost phone loses only unsynced entries.

*Customers and conflicts:*
- Creating a customer with a phone number that already exists in the shop shows "Karma Dorji already has this number. Use them?" instead of creating a duplicate.
- The owner can merge two customers, which moves all entries to one of them. Concurrent edits to a name or phone are resolved last-write-wins by `received_at`.
- A customer with a non-zero balance can't be deleted, only archived.

*Debt aging and sorting:*
- Payments are allocated FIFO against the oldest unpaid credits. A customer's debt age is the number of days since their oldest still-unpaid credit, and it's flagged once it passes 30 days.
- Credit home sorts by amount owed (largest first) by default, with an "Oldest first" toggle.

*Corrections:*
- Tapping an entry in the history and choosing "Correct" creates a reversal entry, and optionally the right entry in its place, in one step. A reason is optional.
- The history shows the original entry struck through, linked to its reversal. Nothing is ever deleted.

*Roles (owner / staff):*
- Both can add customers, record credit and payments, see a customer's balance, and send reminders.
- Owner only: shop total, CSV export, shop settings, inviting and removing staff, merging and archiving customers, and correcting anyone's entry. Staff can correct only their own entries, within 24 hours.
- Removing a staff member signs out their devices on the next sync. Entries they recorded offline before removal are still accepted and flagged for the owner to review.

*Login:*
- The owner signs up with a phone number and a one-time SMS code, needed once per device. After that, a 4-digit PIN unlocks the app, and the session keeps working offline indefinitely.
- The owner can sign out any device from settings; it takes effect on that device's next sync. Staff join via an invite code from the owner, then set their own PIN.
- WhatsApp OTP is the fallback if SMS costs or delivery prove bad (evaluate in /plan-eng-review).

*Tenant isolation and data protection:*
- Every row carries `shop_id`, and isolation is enforced server-side (row-level security), never only in the client. No query can cross shops.
- Customer names, phone numbers and debts are visible only to that shop's users. Support access by the DrukStock team requires the owner's consent and is logged.
- The owner can export all shop data and delete the shop. A customer's deletion request goes through the shop owner.

*Screens:* Credit home (total owed, sort toggle, search by name or phone), Customer detail (keypad + "Gave credit" / "Got payment", history with corrections), WhatsApp reminder deep link prefilled from the shop's template (amount, shop name, mBoB details), Shop settings, Staff management, CSV export.
- Sales, Stock and Reports tabs are visible but disabled.

**Phase 2: GST invoices. Gate: the hardware test in the Phase 0 decision rule.**
- Shop TPN/CID, buyer TPN, 5% GST with price shown before and after tax, inclusive/exclusive pricing per item, per-item exempt flag, and rounding to whole Nu.
- **Invoice numbering:** in v1, invoices are numbered only online. The server assigns the next number per shop atomically, so there are no gaps or duplicates. An invoice started offline is saved as an unnumbered draft and gets its number when the phone is back online. Confirm this approach against DRC guidance before release.
- "Add to credit" posts the invoice total to the customer's credit balance, which links the two modules.
- A PDF invoice shared via WhatsApp.
- Exact invoice fields and rounding rules must be confirmed against DRC guidance before release, because a wrong invoice creates liability for the shop.

**Phase 3: Inventory, sales and summaries.** Only after shops use Phase 1 every day.

**Wireframe (approved, rough):** `/Users/test/Desktop/Druk_Stock/docs/designs/drukstock-wireframe.png` shows three states: Credit home, Customer keypad, and the gated GST invoice. Source: `drukstock-wireframe.html` in the same folder.

## Open Questions
1. Grocers or registered hardware shops first? Decided by the Phase 0 interviews.
2. Do shopkeepers want to send reminders to customers at all, or is that socially awkward in Bhutanese shop culture? Ask directly in interviews.
3. Login: the default is decided (SMS OTP once per device, then a PIN; see Phase 1). Still unverified: SMS cost and delivery reliability on Bhutanese carriers. That decides whether the WhatsApp OTP fallback becomes the primary.
4. Does DRC require e-invoicing or integration with its revenue system for registered businesses? Unverified; check before Phase 2.
5. Pricing: is there a free credit tier with paid GST/staff features? Set the price from what interviewees say they'd pay, not from a guess.
6. Language: English only at launch, or English + Dzongkha labels?
7. For the first hardware shop that wants GST invoices, should the founder run invoices by hand (concierge) for 2 weeks before building Phase 2?

## Success Criteria
- **Phase 0:** 10 interviews done in person. At least 3 of 5 grocers show a credit ledger with debts older than 30 days. Write down the Nu amounts you see.
- **Phase 1:** 2+ shops record credit in DrukStock every working day for 2 weeks. Median entry time under 5 seconds (stopwatch at the counter). The total Nu in debts older than 30 days goes down over those 2 weeks.
  - **If** Phase 0 shows ≥3 of 5 owners are comfortable sending reminders: at least one reminder per shop per week, and some Nu collected that the owner attributes to a reminder.
  - **If not:** reminders stay in the app but aren't measured, and the overdue-debt metric above carries the test.
- **Phase 2 gate:** ≥3 of 5 registered hardware shops would pay Nu 300-500/month for GST invoicing. **Phase 2 success:** 1 shop pays for a month.

## Distribution Plan
- Delivered as a web service: an installable PWA ("Add to Home Screen") at a single URL, so there's no app store review and it works on Android and on computers.
- Hosting: a managed Postgres + auth backend and a static frontend host; to be chosen in /plan-eng-review.
- CI/CD: none exists. Initialize git and a GitHub repo, then auto-deploy on merge to main with a preview deploy per PR.
- Getting users: walk into shops in Thimphu and install it on the owner's phone in person. There's no marketing channel until 10 shops use it.

## Dependencies
- Interview access to 10 Thimphu shops (the founder's network, or walking in).
- The GST Act text and DRC invoice guidance (before Phase 2).
- mBoB or other payment details per shop for reminder messages.
- A git repo (the folder is currently empty and not under version control).

## The Assignment

1. Ask to see the credit (*baki*) book. Count the customers who owe money and how many debts are older than 30 days. Write down the total Nu owed.
2. Ask: "When did you last lose money because someone didn't pay? How much?"
3. At hardware shops, ask: "How do you make GST invoices today? Show me one." Then ask: "Would you pay Nu 400 a month if your phone did this?" Watch their face, not their words.

Bring back 10 pages of notes with numbers. Those numbers decide Phase 2 and the first customer.

### R1-1 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The build-versus-interview sequencing contradicts itself. The Assignment says 'Before writing more than the data model, visit 5 general grocers and 5 GST-registered hardware shops', but the Recommended Approach runs Phase 0 'in parallel with build start' and says Phase 1 (the full credit module with screens, sync and reminders) is 'built now'. Premise #1 says interviews run before any module beyond foundation and credit, which is a third version.

**Remedy**

> Pick one sequencing rule and state it the same way in Premise #1, the Recommended Approach and The Assignment: for example, only the data model and auth before interviews finish, or the whole Phase 1 in parallel. Then name what work is allowed during Phase 0.

### R1-2 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> No decision rule or kill/pivot criterion connects the Phase 0 results to what happens next. Open Question 1 says the interviews decide grocer vs. hardware, but the only thresholds are '3 of 5 grocers show debts older than 30 days' and the Phase 2 gate for hardware. The document never says which segment wins if both or neither pass, or whether Phase 1 continues, changes or stops if the grocer criterion fails.

**Remedy**

> Add explicit outcomes: which segment comes first under each combination of results (both pass, only one passes, neither passes), and whether Phase 1 work continues, pivots or pauses in each case.

### R1-3 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The offline-first multi-device sync is committed but only sketched ('recorded locally and synced', 'resolved append-only'). Required mechanics are missing: client-generated IDs and idempotent upload so retries don't duplicate entries, ordering and timestamps when device clocks differ, and protection against data loss when unsynced entries sit in PWA browser storage that Android can evict or that is lost with the phone. The '2-4 days' CC estimate does not account for this hidden complexity.

**Remedy**

> Specify the sync model: client-side IDs, idempotent server writes, timestamp/ordering policy, how unsynced entries are surfaced to the user, and persistent-storage handling or loss mitigation. Then revisit the effort estimate.

### R1-4 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The append-only conflict rule covers only ledger entries. Customer records can also be created or edited offline on several devices: the same customer may be added twice (e.g. 'Karma'), or a name or phone may be changed concurrently. Merge, duplicate and delete behavior for customers is undefined.

**Remedy**

> Define conflict and duplicate behavior for customer records: how concurrent edits resolve, how duplicates are detected or merged (e.g. by phone number), and whether a customer with a balance can be deleted or archived.

### R1-5 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Corrections are made 'as new reversing entries', but no screen or flow for making a correction is specified. At a busy counter, mistyped amounts and the wrong customer will be common, and this is core to the auditability claim.

**Remedy**

> Add the correction flow to the Phase 1 screens: how a user reverses or corrects an entry, how a reversal is shown in history, and whether it needs a reason.

### R1-6 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The approach commits to owner and staff users on shared financial records but defines no roles or permissions: who can add or remove staff, reverse entries, see shop-wide totals, export CSV (a full customer debt list), or send reminders. Unrestricted staff access to financial records is a correctness and trust risk.

**Remedy**

> Define the owner and staff permission set for each Phase 1 action, plus what happens to a removed staff member's device and their unsynced entries.

### R1-7 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Phase 1 commits to phone-number login, but the method is left as Open Question 3 (SMS OTP cost, WhatsApp OTP, or PIN; unverified). The design also does not say how authentication behaves offline: session lifetime, re-login without connectivity, or a lost or shared phone. Phase 1 cannot be built without these decisions.

**Remedy**

> Choose a Phase 1 login method, or a default with a fallback, and specify the offline session behavior: how long a session lasts offline, PIN lock, and remote sign-out for lost devices.

### R1-8 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> A multi-tenant cloud database will hold customers' names, phone numbers and debts, but the design never states tenant isolation or data-protection requirements: one shop's data must never be readable by another, and customer debt data must be kept private.

**Remedy**

> State the tenant-isolation requirement (e.g. every row scoped to shop_id and enforced server-side) and the basic data-handling commitments (who can access customer data, and export/deletion on request), for /plan-eng-review to implement.

### R1-9 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> 'Flag old debts' is a core pain and Credit home sorts by 'oldest debt', but debt age is undefined. Balances are derived from credit and payment entries, so a debt's age depends on how payments are allocated (FIFO against the oldest credits, or the date of the last payment). The sort is also ambiguous: largest or oldest, as the primary key?

**Remedy**

> Define debt aging (e.g. FIFO payment allocation, with age = date of the oldest unpaid credit) and the default sort order, with any toggle.

### R1-10 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The WhatsApp reminder is prefilled with the shop name and mBoB payment details, and Dependencies lists per-shop mBoB details, but the data model (Shop → users → customers → ledger entries) and screens include no shop settings for capturing them. Customer phone numbers also need a defined format for the WhatsApp deep link.

**Remedy**

> Add shop settings (name, mBoB/payment details, reminder template) to the Phase 1 data model and screens, and specify phone-number capture in international format for wa.me links.

### R1-11 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Phase 1 success requires 'at least one reminder sent per shop per week', but Open Question 2 says it is unknown whether shopkeepers will send reminders at all, because it may be socially awkward. The success criterion assumes the answer to an open question.

**Remedy**

> Make the reminder metric conditional on the Phase 0 answer to Open Question 2, or replace it with a metric that does not presuppose reminders are acceptable.

### R1-12 — feasibility (blocking)

**Remedy**

> Decide how invoice numbers are issued: online-only issuance, per-device number series or prefixes, or provisional numbers finalized on sync. Confirm the choice against DRC guidance.
